Two kinds of people use Clientword

Freelancers create an account. For their own account details we are the data controller.

Clients are invited by a freelancer to a private page. The freelancer decides what to ask and collect, and is the controller of what their clients submit. We act as the freelancer's processor and handle that information only to provide the service. If you are a client and want to see, correct or delete what you submitted, contact the freelancer who invited you.

What we hold

  • Freelancer account: name, business name, email address, a salted hash of your password (never the password itself), your settings, contract template and payment instructions.
  • Client records you create: client name, email, project name, notes, answers to your questions, uploaded files, shared links, messages, deposit status and meeting details.
  • Signed agreements: the agreement text, the signer's typed name, time of signing, IP address and browser details, and a fingerprint (SHA-256) of the text. This is kept as evidence of what was signed.
  • Technical logs: request logs for security and fault finding. Client links are replaced by a placeholder in these logs.

Why we use it

To run your account and the onboarding you set up (contract performance and legitimate interests in operating and securing the service). We send only the emails the service needs: invitations, reminders, notifications, password and email-confirmation links. We do not sell data and do not use it for advertising.

Cookies

We use one cookie, sid, to keep freelancers signed in. It is strictly necessary, so no consent banner is needed. Client pages set no cookies. We use no analytics or advertising trackers.

Who else handles it

We use Amazon Web Services for hosting, database, file storage and email sending, in the [AWS REGION, e.g. London] region. Payments are not processed by Clientword: a deposit request only shows the payment link or instructions the freelancer supplies.

How long we keep it

Until the freelancer deletes the client or their account. Deleting an account removes the account, all its clients, files, messages and signed records. Login sessions expire after 14 days. Backups may hold deleted data for a short period before they expire.

Your rights

You can ask for access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, or object to processing. Freelancers can download their data and delete their account in Setup. You can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Passwords are hashed. Uploaded files are stored privately and are only reachable through short-lived links. No system is perfectly secure, and we will tell affected people and the regulator where the law requires it.
